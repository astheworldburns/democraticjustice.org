On April 15, 2026, Michael Bowman stood at the glass partition inside Worthington Town Hall, camera raised, and asked Mayor Debbie Heflin, "[Do you just, like, are you just, like, do you just lie? Like, it's just, like, lies just come out of your mouth?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MoEdeQIAiG0)"

Heflin did not answer. Bowman told the mayor she was making herself "look like an ass" and accused private citizens of being the mayor's "cronies," saying he knew who her "people are." He said he wanted to "know where the $2 million is going."

Gov. Patrick Morrisey announced a [$2 million AMLER grant](https://governor.wv.gov/article/governor-morrisey-announces-2-million-worthington-sanitary-sewer-rehabilitation) for Worthington in February. At a sewer-board meeting that same month, [the Thrasher Group outlined a four-phase replacement project](https://www.wboy.com/news/marion/sewer-board-discusses-how-far-worthingtons-new-2-million-grant-will-go/), with each phase estimated at $6 million to $8 million. The money Bowman asked about was for Phase I, in the Hutchinson area. Thrasher said it was still waiting for exact instructions on what the $2 million could fund.

Bowman said he was not trying to make Heflin out to be a villain, but that she was "falling right into that role."

Later that day, deputies arrested Bowman on obstruction charges, which were [later dropped](https://www.timeswv.com/news/local_news/marion-county-sheriffs-office-drops-worthington-charges-against-content-creator-michael-bowman/article_1d225329-4b57-46af-9493-42bead816e81.html). On June 5, he filed a [federal lawsuit](https://democraticjustice.org/documents/bowman-v-worthington-complaint.pdf) against the Town of Worthington, Marion County, the county sheriff, various officials, and a private citizen who posted about his arrest on social media. 

Bowman's federal complaint describes him as an independent journalist. The [Society of Professional Journalists' Code of Ethics](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/) identifies four obligations for those claiming journalism's authority: seek truth and report it, minimize harm, act independently, and be accountable and transparent.

## SEEK TRUTH AND REPORT IT

Journalism begins with getting the facts straight. Bowman's public statements and legal filings repeatedly describe events one way, while raw video and audio show something else.

Bowman's federal complaint alleges that a town employee accelerated a truck toward Bowman and swerved away at close range. The video records the employee telling Bowman, "Step back sir so I don't run you over." Bowman later said, "I don't know if he was trying to hit me or not."

Bowman claimed no 911 call existed and implied the mayor used a back channel to summon a "posse." The police dispatch audio, which Bowman himself published, records a female caller asking for an officer and describing Bowman recording and refusing to stop.

In the sidewalk confrontation after the sewer-board meeting, Bowman's published video cuts the moment where he screams "Dip shit. Fuck face!" It also cuts him shouting, "You want to go at it with a fucking Brazilian jiu-jitsu purple belt? I don't give a fuck about nobody."

In voiceover, Bowman invoked "Appalachian stereotypes where you pull into the wrong holler, get out of the car, and end up shot." He said that was unfounded, but said his arrest was the result of "decades of good old boy secrets that were built on loyalty, maintained by fear, and protected through retaliation."

## MINIMIZE HARM

Journalism requires treating subjects with respect. The code warns that "pursuit of the news is not a license for arrogance or undue intrusiveness."

As seen in the town hall confrontation, Bowman said office staff were "weird," asked if lies just come out of her mouth, and said she was making herself look like an ass. When she declined to speak, he called her a villain. He refused to leave and called the situation an "old fashioned standoff."

The night before that encounter, Bowman entered the sewer board meeting after it had begun and recorded items on a side table. He walked to the front of the room and stood between the audience and the board, filming the proceedings for several minutes, then relocated to the side wall and continued to record. He interrupted the meeting, saying, "since there is no public forum… we got to try to discuss in our own way."

During that same meeting, Bowman fixated on a private citizen filming him: "She can't keep her camera off me." Later, "She won't let me look at her." His companion added, "Oh no, that's the one that comes after us." Bowman later named her as one of the mayor's "cronies" and made her the sole private defendant in his federal lawsuit.

According to Bowman's federal complaint, the altercation occurred in the presence of minor children. In footage of the incident, Bowman refers to the employee's children as "his fucking kids."

After his arrest, Bowman directed followers to call the prosecutor, sheriff, and Town Hall, asking for the mayor and demanding the charges be dropped. Town Hall [closed to walk-in traffic](https://www.timeswv.com/news/local_news/marion-county-sheriffs-office-drops-worthington-charges-against-content-creator-michael-bowman/article_1d225329-4b57-46af-9493-42bead816e81.html), citing public statements encouraging hostile visits.

## ACT INDEPENDENTLY

Journalists serve the public. They must avoid factionalism and influencing stories they cover.

In an interview with the Civil Rights Lawyer on YouTube, Bowman said he asked a local contact to "connect and round up the troops with the locals" before the sewer-board meeting. He also said the meeting appealed to him because he needed "B-roll for the video" and wanted to "stress test the environment" to see what response he would receive "as an outside journalist" filming in the chamber.

His own coverage of his arrest opens by telling viewers his arrest was unconstitutional, which is a legal conclusion that no court has reached. He did not label this as an opinion. In that same clip, Bowman claims he had "no choice but to make an example out of" Marion County. A month later, he filed a federal lawsuit seeking damages from the people he was covering.

A $1.99 subscription solicitation accompanies most of his posts. He has stated he is building a name and brand with the intent of making money later. The [West Virginia Democratic Party's public calendar](https://www.wvdemocrats.com/events) bills him by name alongside a congressional candidate at that candidate's sponsored event.

## BE ACCOUNTABLE AND TRANSPARENT

Ethical journalism requires taking responsibility for one's work and explaining one's decisions to the public.

He sued a private citizen who recorded him at the Sewer Board meeting and posted about his arrest on social media, alleging defamation and false light in coordination with state actors. The complaint does not quote the statements it says are false or identify any communication linking her to a public official.

When a former congressional candidate criticized Bowman, he called her "a cancer" and said she represented the worst of what West Virginia has to offer. In a comment thread, he also wrote that he would "block a bitch" in reference to her.

_______

The cost of Bowman's content creation remains to be seen. Worthington [tabled a discussion on dissolving its charter](https://www.wdtv.com/2026/06/17/worthington-town-council-discusses-sewer-project-decides-not-unincorporate-town/) at its June 2026 meeting. Dissolving, sometimes called unincorporating, would likely cause utility rates for Worthington customers to skyrocket for years to come as some 400 customers and residents pay off $20M+ in debt from the state taking over the system.

Worthington shows what state abandonment looks like at ground level. The [EPA's latest survey](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-05/2022-cwns-summary.pdf) placed West Virginia among the nation's highest states for documented clean water needs per resident: $6,182, behind only the Northern Mariana Islands. The agency warned that its count likely understates the need.

West Virginia has many towns like Worthington with profitable narratives for storytellers. Only time can tell if they will also give the rights to their stories away for free when Michael Bowman shows up with a camera.

BowmanTV: Coming soon to a town near you.
